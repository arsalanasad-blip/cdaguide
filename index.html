1 How to use this CDA rules guide (design workflow)

To design a CDA-compliant house on any plot size, follow these seven steps in order. Each step points to the section with exact figures.

1Fix your plot facts — exact size (sq. yd.), frontage (ft), dimensions & orientation from the allotment letter and on-site demarcation. (§2)
2Find your row in the Master Table using both size and frontage — terraced, semi-detached or detached. (§3)
3Draw the buildable block = plot minus compulsory setbacks. That block may be covered on both floors. (§4, §8)
4Set the vertical envelope — 2 storeys, max 30 ft height, plinth cap, mumty on top. (§5, §6)
5Add basement, porch, projections strictly within their own limits. (§7, §9)
6Fit rooms to minimum sizes; place servant quarter, stairs, tanks, trees. (§10, §12)
7Run the pre-submission checklist before your architect files for approval. (§15)

⚡ Shortcut: the companion 📐 Plot Design Calculator → auto-generates steps 2–4 for any plot dimensions.

✅ The single most important concept Under the 2020 Regulations there is no fixed ground-coverage percentage or FAR number for dwelling houses. The rule is: “construction may cover a block, leaving compulsory setbacks” — on ground and first floor (Reg. 2.1.8 & Schedule-1). Your covered area is therefore decided by your setbacks, which depend on plot size + frontage. Old percentage rules (60%/50%) no longer apply.

2 CDA plot categories & Marla–Kanal size conversions

2.1 The three development types

🏘️ Terraced / Attached (Type A)

Houses in a row sharing party walls on both sides. No side setbacks. Plots up to 450 sq. yd. with frontage 20–49 ft. Typical: 5, 7, 8, 10 & 14 marla.

🏠 Semi-Detached (Type B)

Pair of houses sharing one party wall, open yard on the other side. Plots 300–625 sq. yd., frontage 40–49 ft. One side setback of 5 ft.

🏡 Detached (Type C & D)

Standalone house, open on all four sides. Plots 400–2,720 sq. yd., frontage 50 ft and above. Typical: 1 kanal and above.

📌 Type is decided by size + frontage together Schedule-1 Note (vii): the development type of a plot is determined considering both size and frontage. If your plot's size and frontage fall in different rows (e.g. large area but narrow frontage), CDA Building Control decides the applicable row — confirm before designing. For irregular plots, size/frontage = average of front and rear dimensions (Note iv).

4 CDA covered area rules + worked examples

4.1 The formula (all dwelling houses)

Block
= Plot minus compulsory setbacks (all sides)
GF + FF
= The block may be fully covered on BOTH floors (Reg. 2.1.8)
+ Mumty
= ¼ of GF block (detached: capped at 600 sft)
+ Basement
= Extra, NOT counted in permissible covered area
✅ What counts / does not count Counted: all roofed floor areas incl. verandahs, bay windows, porch area falling inside the block, 25% of pergola area, stair halls. Measured externally (wall to wall).  NOT counted: basement/cellar/vault area, roof projections (chajja) within Schedule-3/4 limits, projections over setbacks within limits. Basement area is still counted for scrutiny-fee and property-tax calculation.
⛔ Excess covered area = demolition Reg. 2.4.2 / 4.1.7: any covered area beyond Schedule-1 limits shall be demolished — it cannot be regularized by paying a fine. Only minor deviations up to 5%, with prior Authority approval, may be compounded with charges (Reg. 2.4.3). Design inside the envelope.

✅ The basement advantage Basement area is not counted in FAR / permissible covered area and not counted as a storey (Reg. 2.6.1(ii)(xix)) — but it is counted for scrutiny fee and property tax. Detached houses may build basement under 100% of the permissible ground-floor coverage (Reg. 2.6.1(ix)).
ItemRule
LocationOnly under the plinth area, leaving all minimum setbacks. Never inside setbacks — except a driveway bridge over natural depression, or storage under the driveway where depression exceeds 8′.

13 Extra, adjoining & excess CDA land — purchase and use rules

The 2020 Building Control Regulations contain no separate “extra land” chapter — but several binding rules govern every kind of land beyond your allotted plot. They boil down to one principle: you may build only inside your demarcated plot; everything outside it belongs to CDA/the state.

1️⃣ Build only inside your plot line

Plans must mark the property/boundary line on every floor plan (basement, GF, FF, mumty). Setbacks are measured from the plot line; the boundary wall stands on the plot line — not beyond it. Any structure outside = encroachment, liable to removal.

2️⃣ “Excess area” inside your plot

If demarcation shows your plot physically measures more than the allotted area, the excess must be regularized and paid for through the CDA Estate Wing (at the prevailing schedule) before your building plan can be approved. Get demarcation done first; confirm current rates at the One Window Operation.

3️⃣ Adjoining open / green / nullah land

Green belts, road berms, nullah edges, parks and vacant CDA strips next to (especially corner) houses are state land: no construction, no boundary-wall extension, no permanent enclosure, no services on it. CDA removes encroachments at the owner's risk and cost — and courts have barred carving private plots out of greenbelts/open spaces (IHC, Aug 2024).

4️⃣ The benefit you CAN take

A side facing an open space/nullah (not a road) earns up to 50% relaxation of that side's setback (§8.1) — the Regulations' formal way of rewarding open surroundings instead of occupying them.

5️⃣ Corner plots

A plot is a “corner plot” only if declared so by CDA (intersection of two vehicular streets). Either street may count as the front. Corner location carries premium/location charges at allotment/transfer per CDA's schedule; in return, plots >300 sq. yd. get the extra 150 sft rear porch (§9.1).

6️⃣ Street green belt & trees

You must plant and maintain 1–5 trees in the green belt along the street (§12) — but this gives no ownership or enclosure rights; the belt stays public, and no tree may be cut without permission.

⛔ Common expensive mistakes Extending the lawn/boundary over adjoining CDA land · building a guard room, generator room or ramp on the berm · assuming a “private” side strip is yours because you maintain it · buying a plot assuming the empty land beside it can be merged without CDA disposal/allotment. All are encroachments: CDA can reclaim the land and demolish the structure. Verify plot boundaries by official demarcation (Amin report) before purchase and before design.

15 Pre-submission CDA design checklist for architects

  • Plot size, frontage, dimensions & orientation taken from allotment letter + fresh demarcation (no assumed “extra” land).
  • Correct Master-Table row applied using size + frontage; irregular-plot averaging done if needed.
  • Buildable block = plot minus all setbacks; smaller side setback on south/west; GF & FF inside block.
  • Only 2 storeys; height ≤ 30′ from mean road level; reads as 2-storey from front; plinth ≤ 3′-6″/5′-0″.
  • Mumty ≤ ¼ GF block (≤600 sft detached); width ≤ ½ / ⅓ plot width; depth > width; ≤10′ high; stair + 1 room + 1 bath only; no kitchen/unit.
  • Basement under plinth area only, setbacks left; 5′ neighbour offset or NOCs for terraced; 8′-6″–12′ clear; mechanical sewage + storm disposal; 3′×3′ emergency exit.
  • Porch within area + ⅔-setback + length caps; open sides correct; no habitation under it; drainage inside plot.
  • All projections ≤ Schedule-3/4 limits and ≤ ½ setback width; balcony/bay-window/pergola/open-stair rules met.
  • All rooms meet Schedule-2 minima (100/45/24 sft…); doors 7′; basement habitation compliant; OTS ≥50 sft.
  • Units ≤ 1 (<488) / 2 (≥488); servant room + bath if ≥501 sq. yd.; home office ≤2 rooms/25% with permission.
  • Boundary wall 3′–7′ on plot line; parapet 3′; tanks set back & unobtrusive; no mobile antenna; pools only if permitted.
  • Rainwater harvesting shown (≥400 sq. yd.); trees 1–5 per frontage on plans; sewer + storm connections to CDA mains.
  • Nothing outside the plot line; setback-facing-open-space relaxation claimed formally if applicable.
  • Drawings complete (plans/sections/elevations/schedules/levels/stairs/trees/disposal), signed & stamped; fees ready; neighbour NOCs / bank guarantees where triggered.

💡 Worked example — 4-kanal farm (min. size) 4 kanal = 2,420 sq. yd. = 21,780 sft → 20% = 4,356 sft per floor (basement + ground + first). Total roofed ≈ 4,356 (GF) + 4,356 (FF) + 450 (mumty) = ≈ 9,162 sft + 140 sft guard room. On a square-ish plot (≈147′ × 148′), setbacks leave ≈117′ × 43′ ≈ 5,031 sft — so the 20% cap binds, not the setbacks. On narrow/deep shapes, check both.

17.3 Sub-division saga — current status: BARRED

⛔ Do not buy half a farm without reading this 2020 Regulations: “No sub-division and amalgamation shall be allowed.”  Aug 2023: an amendment appeared to allow one sub-division of 5-acre plots into 2 × 2.5 acres (coverage split 4,750/4,750, no increase).  Oct 2024: the CDA Board ruled there was no provision for sub-division of agro farms in any scheme, barred it, and ordered an inquiry into the 2023 change. Current position: sub-division barred. ~150 of ~550 farms were sub-divided historically — their status is case-by-case. Verify the individual farm's record at CDA before any transaction.
